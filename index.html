01 / The idea

A chain of thought
is rarely a chain.

Long reasoning traces loop back, test intermediate claims, recover from mistakes, and reuse ideas introduced much earlier.

ReasoningFlow represents this hidden organization as a directed acyclic graph: text spans become semantic nodes, while labeled edges capture how one step plans, reasons about, or evaluates another.

03 / What the graph reveals

Four findings,
made visible.

The graph is more than a visualization. It makes testable structural questions possible.

01
Local verification

Models check nearby claims—not only final answers.

Reasoning models repeatedly verify intermediate facts, restatements, and deductions. These local checks appear far more often than whole-answer verification, exposing a fine-grained error-control process inside long traces.

02
Self-reflection

Reflection signals are useful—but not perfectly calibrated.

Positive reflections occur most often around valid reasoning; uncertain and negative reflections are increasingly associated with errors. The graph lets us measure where confidence language agrees—or disagrees—with step correctness.

03
Answer connectivity

Not every mistake reaches the answer.

A step can be wrong yet structurally abandoned. By tracing reachability to conclusion nodes, ReasoningFlow distinguishes harmless dead ends from errors that remain connected to—and may compromise—the final answer.

04
Mechanistic interpretability

The annotated edges partially align with model influence.

Black-box influence signals recover some human-annotated dependencies above chance. The alignment is modest—not a claim of complete faithfulness—but it connects semantic graph structure to relationships detectable from model behavior.
