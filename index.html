ReasoningFlow

Semantic Structure of Complex Reasoning Traces

Jinu Lee, Sagnik Mukherjee, Dilek Hakkani-Tur, and Julia Hockenmaier

A unified schema that represents long, nonlinear reasoning traces as labeled directed acyclic graphs, making planning, verification, reflection, and backtracking explicit.

Paper Code Dataset Talk

Overview

Reasoning models rarely proceed as simple chains. They introduce plans, reuse earlier facts, inspect intermediate results, abandon branches, and revise conclusions.

ReasoningFlow segments a trace into semantic units and connects them with labeled relations. The resulting graph supports structural queries over the reasoning process and separates the order in which text was written from the dependencies between steps.

Findings

Four observations enabled by the graph representation.

01

Local verification

Reasoning models check intermediate facts, restatements, and deductions much more often than they re-evaluate a complete final answer. The graph exposes this fine-grained error-control process.

02

Self-reflection

Positive reflections are most often associated with valid reasoning, while uncertain and negative reflections are increasingly enriched around errors. These signals are useful, but not perfectly calibrated.

positive uncertain negative
03

Answer connectivity

An erroneous step does not necessarily corrupt the final answer. Reachability in the DAG distinguishes errors on abandoned branches from errors that remain connected to a conclusion.

errordead end
errorreasonanswer
04

Mechanistic interpretability

Black-box influence signals recover some annotated dependencies above chance. The alignment is modest, but connects human-interpretable graph edges to relationships detectable from model behavior.
